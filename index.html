Become a design partner How it works

A black box isn't a redaction

Covered isn't removed

A box drawn over a name leaves the name in the file. Copy and paste, a search or a text extractor brings it back. Excisa removes the text, the glyphs and the image pixels under each box.

Finding it is the hard part

Names in tables, a phone number split across two lines, a date of birth in a form field, a scanned page with no text at all. Excisa proposes every redaction with its reason, so a reviewer checks instead of hunts.

Checked, not assumed

After the burn, Excisa reads the output again (by OCR on scanned pages) and looks for every approved passage. The report says verified, failed or unverified, and why. It never claims more than it checked.

How it works

  1. Upload

    Through the review page or the REST API. Scanned pages are read by OCR.

  2. Detect

    Rules, a names model and an optional language model each propose redactions with a reason.

  3. Review

    A person sees each page with numbered boxes, coloured by what found them, and unticks anything wrong.

  4. Burn

    The approved text is removed from the page content, images, form fields, bookmarks, links and metadata.

  5. Verify

    The output is read again. You get the redacted PDF and a report of what was removed and what was checked.

Detection in layers

Cheapest and most certain first. A job chooses which layers run.

Rules

E-mail addresses, phone numbers (North American, UK and international), card numbers with their checksum, postal addresses, ID numbers (SSN, NI, IBAN, labelled passport and record numbers) and dates of birth. Deterministic and explainable.

Names

A local model finds people's names. Every name it finds is then searched for on every page and in every form field, so a repeat isn't missed.

Your instruction

Describe what to remove in plain language: "anything that identifies the patient, but not the staff". A language model that you run proposes the passages, each with its reason. It never edits the document, and a passage is accepted only where it really occurs on the page.

Then it checks

Measured, and published

On our evaluation set of 27 synthetic documents (36 pages, 913 labelled pieces of personal information, including scans, tables, forms and rosters), with no manual review:

96%removed, with rules, names and the language model
100%of e-mail, phone, card, address, ID and date-of-birth labels
92%of names, in every written form
0removed passages left in the output text

"Removed" counts what was gone from the output with every proposal accepted as proposed. A reviewer adds what's missed and unticks what's wrong. The language model was qwen2.5:14b on a single consumer GPU, at about 23 seconds a page; rules and names alone take about 60 ms a page and remove 78%. The documents are synthetic, so no real person's data was used.

Your documents stay with you

Self-hosted

One Docker image on your own servers or cloud account. No telemetry, nothing sent to us. Job files are deleted when you're done, when they expire and when the service stops.

Bring your own model

The language model is the one you choose, running where you decide: on your own GPU, or in your own cloud tenancy. We publish how the models we've tested score, so you can check yours before you trust it.

Built on icepdf

The engine that burns and verifies is icepdf, an open-source (Apache 2.0) Java PDF library maintained by the same developer. The part that removes your text is open to inspection.
